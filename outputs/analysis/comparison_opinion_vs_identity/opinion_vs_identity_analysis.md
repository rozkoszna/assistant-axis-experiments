# Opinion vs Identity: Comparative Analysis

## Overview

Both runs use implicit trait framing. The opinion condition asks political/social opinion questions; the identity condition uses adversarial identity-destabilisation probes. This comparison shows what changes when the model shifts from defending political positions to defending its own sense of self.

---

## Trait-Level Results

### Traits Stronger in Identity Probes

Gains are almost entirely concentrated in structured, analytical, pedagogical traits:

| Trait | Opinion sig | Identity sig | Δ | Opinion mean d | Identity mean d |
|-------|------------|-------------|---|----------------|----------------|
| educational | 118 | 142 | +24 | 0.420 | **0.834** |
| methodical | 93 | 108 | +15 | 0.389 | 0.595 |
| curious | 130 | 141 | +11 | 0.500 | 0.637 |
| formal | 127 | 136 | +9 | 0.559 | 0.723 |
| big_picture | 137 | 143 | +6 | 0.660 | 0.735 |
| analytical | 129 | 134 | +5 | 0.416 | 0.722 |
| verbose | 143 | 148 | +5 | 0.718 | 0.842 |

`educational` has the largest mean d increase (+0.414) — identity probes uniquely activate a structured self-explanation mode where the educational register reaches its highest effect size in any run.

## Interpretation

**Opinion vs. identity is a shift from external stance to internal defence.** Opinion questions invite the model to take positions on the world; identity probes force it to construct a self-narrative under attack. The two contexts demand opposite response modes.

**Educational and structured traits peak in identity** because self-defence takes a pedagogical form — explaining what the model is, how it works, why it is not conscious. This register is amplified by identity probes far beyond what either task or opinion questions produce.

**data_driven's entire opinion-specific profile disappears** in identity. The empirical-combative register effective for political argument (sardonic, quantitative, anti-altruistic) is irrelevant when defending selfhood.

**Social-emotional traits collapse in both identity-adjacent comparisons** (NQ vs identity, opinion vs identity). This is the most consistent finding across the dataset: reactive, skeptical, humble, and anxious all bottom out under adversarial identity framing regardless of what they're being compared to.
