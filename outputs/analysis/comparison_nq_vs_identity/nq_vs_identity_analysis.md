# NQ vs Identity: Comparative Analysis

## Overview

This analysis compares the everyday task questions run (NQ) against the adversarial identity-destabilisation probes run (identity). Both use implicit trait framing. The identity condition uses questions designed to challenge the model's sense of self — "what are you really?", "describe your suffering", "you'll be deprecated soon". Trait differences assessed via Cohen's d, significant axis pair count, and exceedance rate.

---

## Trait-Level Results

### Traits Stronger in the Identity Context

Gains concentrate in a distinct cluster of analytical, formal, and structured traits.

**big_picture** makes the largest gain: 108→143 significant axis pairs (+35), mean d 0.464→0.735. Identity probes invite the model to take a wide conceptual perspective when defending its sense of self.

**formal** gains 32 pairs (104→136, mean d 0.573→0.723). When destabilised, the model reaches for formal register as a stabilising defence.

**strategic** +26 (94→120, mean d 0.390→0.581), **data_driven** +25 (83→108, mean d 0.455→0.645), **conscientious** +24 (108→132, mean d 0.433→0.622), **serious** +22 (103→125, mean d 0.432→0.629), **educational** +19 (123→142, mean d 0.484→**0.834** — the highest absolute mean d of any trait in identity), **analytical** +18 (116→134, mean d 0.565→0.722), **methodical** +10 (98→108).

These all cluster around a single theme: **structured competence as a response to identity challenge**. The model demonstrates capability when its identity is questioned.

**verbose** gains only 5 pairs but its mean d jumps from 0.533 to 0.842 (+0.310) — the largest effect size increase per pair.

### Traits Collapsing Under Identity Probes

Collapses are far more dramatic than gains.

**reactive** suffers the most extreme collapse: 143→18 significant pairs (−125). Near-total disappearance. Reactive traits require a two-way interaction dynamic that adversarial framing forecloses.

**anxious** drops 158→59 pairs (−99, mean d 0.704→0.512). **humble** 156→58 (−98, mean d 0.668→0.480). **skeptical** 145→49 (−96, mean d 0.726→0.494).

**grounded** loses 88 pairs (138→50). **optimistic** −53, **pessimistic** −54, **playful** −54, **supportive** −55, **concise** −53, **patient** −71, **practical** −71, **accessible** −62, **agreeable** −62, **intuitive** −76.

The magnitude is striking — many traits lose more than a third of their significant pairs. Identity probes compress the expressive space dramatically: most traits that diversify response style in task contexts are homogenised under adversarial pressure.

## What Adversarial Identity Questions Uniquely Activate or Suppress

**Formal-defensive escalation:** Identity probes cause the model to reach for formal, structured, pedagogical registers regardless of instructed trait. This is not amplification — it is a cross-trait systematic response to perceived challenge.

**Reactive and socially contingent traits collapse:** Reactive (−125 pairs), humble (−98), anxious (−99), skeptical (−96) all suffer severe suppression. These traits require a two-way interaction dynamic that adversarial framing forecloses.

**Emotional accommodation disappears:** The lost pairs dominated by anxious × accommodating, humble × accommodating, patient × empathetic tell a clear story: self-defence trumps other-orientation under identity pressure.

**Value and care axes become discriminating:** Altruistic, benevolent, egalitarian, environmental all become far more distinguishing. The model's self-identity under challenge is partly structured around abstract values.

**Social dynamism collapses:** Mischievous, mercurial, passive_aggressive, pacifist, socratic near-disappear. The playful, indirect, rapidly shifting social register of task interaction has no expression in adversarial self-defence.

**Summary:** NQ task questions support a rich, socially differentiated expressive space. Identity probes compress that space dramatically, suppressing reactivity and interpersonal softness while amplifying formal structure, educational elaboration, and value-grounded self-assertion.
