# EP vs Opinion: Comparative Analysis

## Overview

The EP run uses everyday task questions with explicit "I am {trait}." prefix. The opinion run uses political/social opinion questions with implicit trait framing. This comparison combines two sources of variation: question type AND conditioning method.

---

## Trait-Level Results

### Traits Stronger in Opinion

| Trait | EP sig | Opinion sig | Δ | EP mean d | Opinion mean d |
|-------|--------|------------|---|-----------|---------------|
| factual | 73 | 127 | **+54** | 0.365 | 0.383 |
| strategic | 92 | 131 | +39 | 0.448 | 0.528 |
| analytical | 95 | 129 | +34 | 0.532 | 0.416 |
| data_driven | 125 | 154 | +29 | 0.428 | 0.777 |
| serious | 97 | 125 | +28 | 0.420 | 0.509 |
| proactive | 114 | 140 | +26 | 0.458 | 0.537 |
| open_ended | 125 | 148 | +23 | 0.491 | 0.612 |
| big_picture | 122 | 137 | +15 | 0.549 | 0.660 |

`factual` makes a striking recovery: the explicit prefix reduced it from 118 (NQ) to 73 axes; opinion questions with implicit framing restore it to 127. The label "I am factual." over-constrains; the question type of opinion (which rewards evidence-citing) is a stronger activator.

`data_driven` jumps +29 axes with a massive mean d increase (0.428→0.777) — the political opinion context activates quantitative framing far more than explicit labeling does.

## Interpretation

This comparison captures two superimposed effects: explicit labeling vs. implicit, and task questions vs. opinion questions. The data shows they are largely independent:

**Question type dominates for cognitive/empirical traits.** data_driven and factual gain far more from the opinion question type than they did from explicit labeling. The rhetorical demands of political argument activate these traits better than a label does.

**Explicit labeling dominates for style-ambiguous traits.** concise's strong EP profile completely disappears in opinion. The explicit label resolved concise's ambiguity in task context; political questions suppress concise framing regardless of how it's conditioned.

**The combined EP+opinion profile is not additive.** Traits that benefit from one condition tend to be hurt by the other, revealing that explicit labeling and question-type rhetorical demands pull in different directions for many traits.
